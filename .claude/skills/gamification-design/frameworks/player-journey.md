# The 4 Phases of the Player Journey

A product is not a single static experience. User motivation changes completely from Day 1 to Day 100. Every experience must be designed across four distinct phases.

[DISCOVERY] ──► [ONBOARDING] ──► [SCAFFOLDING] ──► [ENDGAME]
Why try it?     How do I play?   Why continue?     Why stay post-mastery?

---

## Phase 1: Discovery
**Core Question:** *"Why should I even give this my time?"*

- **User State:** Skeptical, curious, uncommitted. Low cognitive threshold.
- **Primary Drives:** CD1 (Meaning/Calling), CD5 (Social Proof), CD7 (Curiosity), CD6 (Exclusivity).
- **Design Goals:**
  - Clearly communicate the higher vision or unique value proposition within 4 seconds.
  - Eliminate friction during account creation or first access.
  - Leverage social proof or exclusive invites.
- **Key Metric:** Sign-up / Trial Conversion Rate.

## Phase 2: Onboarding
**Core Question:** *"How do I understand this and feel competent?"*

- **User State:** Vulnerable to feeling dumb, seeking fast validation.
- **Primary Drives:** CD2 (Accomplishment), CD3 (Immediate Feedback), CD1 (Beginner's Luck).
- **Design Goals:**
  - Guide the user directly to the **"Aha! Moment"** (First Major Win-State).
  - Use visual guidance (Desert Oasis / Glowing Choice) instead of wall-of-text tutorials.
  - Ensure early small challenges yield immediate, gratifying feedback.
- **Key Metric:** Onboarding Completion Rate / Day-1 Retention.

## Phase 3: Scaffolding
**Core Question:** *"Why do I incorporate this into my regular routine?"*

- **User State:** Familiar with basic rules; building habits or seeking systemic depth.
- **Primary Drives:** CD2 (Progression), CD4 (Ownership/Investment), CD5 (Collaboration/Status), CD6 (Routine).
- **Design Goals:**
  - Build repeatable motivation loops (Trigger → Action → Feedback → Reward → Progress).
  - Accumulate user investment (data, profile customization, stats) to build switching costs.
  - Introduce dynamic difficulty matching skill growth (Flow State).
- **Key Metric:** WAU/MAU Ratio, Habit Velocity, Feature Depth.

## Phase 4: Endgame
**Core Question:** *"Why stay after I have mastered everything?"*

- **User State:** Expert, veteran, prone to boredom or burnout if content runs dry.
- **Primary Drives:** CD3 (Autonomy & Strategy), CD5 (Mentorship/Status), CD1 (Legacy/Community Leadership), CD4 (Governance).
- **Design Goals:**
  - Provide **Evergreen Mechanics** where users generate content, strategies, or community value for each other.
  - Transition experts into mentors, moderators, or elite strategists.
  - Avoid simply inflating numerical difficulty (e.g., higher health bars).
- **Key Metric:** Year-1+ Retention, Community Contribution Rate, Churn Rate of Veterans.
